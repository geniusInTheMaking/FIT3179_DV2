Australia, Between the Pages

Exploring who reads, what Australians borrow, and how books connect communities.

Introduction

Every page tells a story. From magical worlds to gripping mysteries, books take us places and bring us together. But what gets Australia reading? Which genres appeal to different generations? And does where we live shape what we love to read?

Australia, Beyond the Pages explores how reading habits vary across Australian generations and communities. Using national reading trends and public library data, it reveals patterns in reading participation, library engagement, and book preferences.

01 - The People Behind the Pages

This section explore how age & gender influence reading habits.

The older we get, the more we read?

Reading participation rises sharply across the age groups surveyed. Australians aged 78+ recorded a participation rate more than four times that of Gen Z, revealing a striking generational divide in leisure reading.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics (2024)

Is age really the reason?

More time for leisure

Older Australians may have fewer work and family commitments after retirement, creating more time for leisure reading.

Different leisure habits

Younger Australians divide their leisure time between streaming, gaming, social media, and other activities, while older generations may spend more time reading.

Who spends longer reading: Men or Women?

Women recorded longer average daily reading times across every generation surveyed. The widest difference appears among Millennials.

Statistics represent the average number of hours spent on leisure reading by male and female survey respondents.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics (2024)

02 - Reading Across Communities

Across Australia, public libraries connect communities with books, resources and shared spaces. Yet the ways people engage with these services differ from place to place.

From library membership and borrowing activity to physical visits and digital collections, this chapter explores how public library engagement varies across Australian communities.

Does Library Membership Vary Across States?

Library membership rates vary across the three states, with New South Wales recording the highest rate (34%) & Western Australia the lowest (22%). This 12% point gap highlights differences in library registration

relative to population. However, membership does not capture all library use. Residents can still visit libraries for study, events and community services without being registered members.

03 - The Books We Choose

Beyond library engagement, the books Australians choose reveal diverse interests and reading preferences.

This chapter explores what Australians read, how preferences differ across audiences, and how borrowing trends change over time.

What Types of Literature Do Australians Read?

Novels are the most widely read category, suggesting that storytelling and entertainment remain key reading motivations among Australians.

Crime and creative non-fiction also attract substantial readership, suggesting that Australians value books for exploring real-world events, understanding human behaviour and gaining insights from personal experiences.

Comics and poetry attract smaller audiences, potentially reflecting more specialised interests rather than a lack of cultural significance.

Obtained data from Source: Creative Australia (2025), National Arts Participation Survey . Visualisation by author.

Do Different Audiences Borrow Different Books?

The heatmap compares the percentage share of selected library checkouts across juveniles, young adults and adults in Brisbane public libraries.

Younger readers may prefer picture books and graphic novels, while older readers may choose more fiction and non-fiction. These differences show how public libraries cater to different age groups by offering a wide range of reading materials.

Hover over each cell to explore checkout counts.

Source: Brisbane City Council, Library Checkout Data, 2024–2026.

Reading Beyond English

Australia's cultural diversity is reflected in the range of languages available in public libraries. This butterfly chart compares book checkouts across five languages in 2024 and 2026, highlighting changes in borrowing preferences.

Chinese-language books recorded the highest checkout numbers among these five languages in both years, while Spanish and Korean were the only languages to record increases.

Source: Brisbane City Council, Library Checkout Data, 2024–2026.

04 - How Do Australians Discover Books?

Book choices are influenced by how readers discover new stories, from personal recommendations and bookshops to libraries and social media.

As digital platforms reshape book discovery, do traditional recommendations still hold more influence than online sources?

Where Do Australians Find Their Next Read?

The popularity of word of mouth may reflect readers' preference for recommendations from people who understand their interests. Unlike social media, where book suggestions compete with large volumes of content, personal recommendations can offer a more targeted approach to discovery. This suggests that trust and personal relevance may matter more than online visibility when choosing what to read next.

Respondents could select multiple discovery methods, so percentages do not add up to 100%.

Source: Creative Australia, National Arts Participation Survey (2025).

Conclusion

Australia's reading habits are changing, but the importance of reading extends beyond the books themselves. From older generations showing greater reading participation to word of mouth influencing 39% of book discoveries, the data reveals how age and personal connections shape reading choices.

Meanwhile, libraries continue to connect communities through both physical and digital services. Together, these patterns suggest that the future of reading is not simply about choosing between print and digital, but about making books accessible and meaningful to different generations and communities.
